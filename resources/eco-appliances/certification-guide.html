Global Certification Comparison: PSE / ETL / KC / CE for Electric Food Waste Disposers

August 2026 · BESTAR Team

Certification is usually the single biggest blocker between an OEM sample and a shippable electric food waste disposer SKU. Here's what each major market actually requires.

MarketMandatory markVoltageNotes
JapanPSE100V 50/60HzRequired to legally sell any electrical appliance in Japan.
North AmericaETL or UL110–120V 60HzRetailers in the US/Canada generally require one or the other before listing.
KoreaKC220V 60HzMandatory safety certification for the Korean market.
EuropeCE + ERP220–240V 50HzCE covers safety/EMC; ERP (energy-related products) covers energy-labeling compliance.
TaiwanBSMI / CNS110V 60HzRequired for electrical goods sold in Taiwan.

Why this is where OEM sourcing saves the most time

Each of these certifications typically requires its own lab testing cycle, and running them in parallel from scratch for a new electric kitchen composter design can take months per market. Sourcing from a manufacturer whose electric food cycler range already holds PSE, ETL/UL, KC, and CE/ERP by model means an OEM brand can sequence market entry — say, Korea first, then Japan — without re-running compliance testing for each launch.

What to confirm before you commit to a market

Ask us which specific model variants hold which certifications — certification is usually tied to a specific voltage/frequency configuration, not the product family as a whole. A 5.5L flagship certified for the Korean 220V/60Hz market isn't automatically certified for the US 110V/60Hz market without its own variant and test cycle.

What is PoE (Power over Ethernet) and Why Your Smart Home Control Panel Needs It?

Published November 2025 · BESTAR Team

Anyone specifying a wall-mounted digital display or smart-home control panel eventually runs into the same installation problem: getting both power and network data to a wall location that may not have an outlet nearby. PoE solves this, and it's why more interactive digital signage OEM buyers are specifying it as standard.

What PoE actually does

Power over Ethernet delivers electrical power and network connectivity over a single Ethernet cable. Instead of running a power cord to an outlet and a separate Ethernet or WiFi connection, an installer pulls one cable from a PoE-enabled network switch to the panel. The panel gets both power and data from that one connection.

Why this matters for smart-home control panels

Smart-home control panels are frequently installed inside a wall cavity, beside a door, or in a hallway — locations chosen for convenience of use, not proximity to a power outlet. PoE means an installer only needs to run one cable during the electrical rough-in phase, rather than coordinating both a power outlet and a data drop at the exact same spot.

Where it shows up in commercial digital signage

The same logic applies at scale: a building standardizing on wall-mounted digital signage for wayfinding, room booking, or conference-room panels across dozens of rooms saves meaningful electrician time by running PoE Ethernet only, rather than power-plus-data to every panel location. BESTAR's 15.6″ PoE Interactive Display is built specifically for this use case, running Android 11 with WiFi fallback and NFC/RFID for access-control workflows.

What to check before specifying PoE hardware

Confirm your network switch supports the PoE class the panel needs (PoE, PoE+, or PoE++ depending on display size and power draw), and check that your cable run length stays within Ethernet's power-delivery limit (100 meters for standard PoE). Beyond that, PoE panels install and operate like any other network device.

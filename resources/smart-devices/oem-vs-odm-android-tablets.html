OEM vs. ODM Android Tablets: Which Is Best for Your Software Project?

Published June 2026 · BESTAR Team

Software teams sourcing a custom Android tablet manufacturer for the first time usually ask the same question early: do we need OEM or ODM? The short answer is that most projects need OEM, and only a minority genuinely need full ODM — but picking wrong in either direction costs real time and money.

OEM: Light Customization on an existing platform

OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturing, in the "light customization" sense most B2B buyers mean) takes an existing, already-certified Android tablet PC and adapts branding, boot logo, packaging, and firmware configuration. Nothing about the underlying hardware changes. This is the right fit for the large majority of white-label Android tablet projects — education programs, POS deployments, kiosk software — where the platform already fits and the goal is a branded, ready-to-ship product fast.

ODM: Full development from your specification

ODM (Original Design Manufacturing) means building a device from your requirement definition — a specific screen size not in the current catalog, a particular sensor combination, a custom enclosure. This runs through BESTAR's full engineering cycle: requirement definition, feasibility, EVT, DVT, PVT, then mass production, protected by an NNN (Non-Use, Non-Disclosure, Non-Circumvention) agreement before any design work starts. It costs more and takes longer, but it's the only path when the hardware itself needs to change.

A simple test to pick between them

Ask: "does an existing tablet PC or digital signage model already meet 90% of my spec?" If yes, OEM light customization gets you there fastest. If your requirement genuinely can't be met by any existing SKU — a screen size we don't carry, a sensor we don't include, an enclosure shape unlike anything in the catalog — that's when full ODM is worth the extra time and cost.

What this looks like on BESTAR's four-tier model

BESTAR frames this as Tier 2 (Light OEM) versus Tier 3 (Full ODM/OEM) on our Solutions page. Most software partners start with a Tier 2 sample from our Tablet PC range to validate their app on real hardware, then decide whether Tier 3 development is worth it once they understand exactly where the standard platform falls short.

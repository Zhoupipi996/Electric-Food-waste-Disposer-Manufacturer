Understanding GMS Certification for B2B Android Tablets

Published June 2026 · BESTAR Team

If you're sourcing an OEM Android tablet PC for a business deployment, one of the first technical questions to settle is whether you need GMS certification — and it isn't automatic on every custom Android tablet.

What GMS certification actually means

Google Mobile Services (GMS) certification means a specific hardware-and-firmware build has passed Google's Compatibility Test Suite (CTS) and is licensed to ship with the Play Store, Gmail, Google Maps, and other Google apps pre-installed. It's a per-build certification, not a blanket approval for a manufacturer — every SKU and firmware revision needs its own sign-off.

Why it matters for B2B buyers

If your end users will install apps from the Play Store, use Google login/SSO, or rely on Google Maps or push notifications through Firebase, you need a GMS-certified build. Education tablets, field-service devices with third-party apps, and consumer-facing kiosks usually fall into this category. On the BESTAR range, models like the 11.97″ Android 13 Tablet ship GMS-certified as standard.

When you can skip it

Single-purpose deployments — a digital menu board, a POS terminal running one locked-down app, a factory-floor HMI panel — often don't need Play Store access at all. Skipping GMS can lower unit cost and simplify firmware, since there's no Google compatibility testing to pass. Most of BESTAR's Interactive Digital Signage line is specified this way.

Why it can't be added later

GMS certification is tied to a specific hardware and firmware combination submitted to Google as a unit. A custom Android tablet manufacturer can't bolt GMS onto an already-shipped, uncertified build after the fact — if there's any chance you'll need it, specify it at the OEM/ODM planning stage, not after mass production has started.

How BESTAR handles it

We confirm GMS requirements during the requirement-definition step of our four-tier engagement model, before quoting. If you're not sure yet, it's a five-minute question to answer on a call — better resolved before tooling than after.
